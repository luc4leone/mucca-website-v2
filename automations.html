The ones that survive are the ones nobody has to remember.

Each of these hangs off work the team was already doing, writes its failures where the team is already looking, and is safe to leave running unattended. Trello → SMS appointment confirmation

Trello · n8n · Twilio · September 2026

A no-show costs the full value of the slot. A reminder costs seven cents.
Problem
A team confirms appointments by hand. Somebody checks the board, sees a booking is confirmed, opens WhatsApp and types a message. It works until somebody is off sick, or forgets, or confirms at 6pm and sends the text the next morning. The customer turns up on the wrong day, or not at all.
The trigger
This is the part that matters, not the SMS. The team already drags the card to Confirmed as part of their existing job. That drag is the only signal the automation needs — nobody learns a new tool, ticks a new box, or remembers a new step. There is no habit to abandon: if they stop moving cards, they have stopped running their business.
What it does
Trello calls n8n the moment a card moves, so the text arrives in about two seconds and nothing polls an API all day to be told nothing happened. The flow then reads the card — the webhook names it but doesn't carry its description or labels — pulls the phone number out, normalises it, and sends. The message carries the card title, so the customer gets the date and time the team actually wrote down, not a generic template.
Safe unattended
Two texts are worse than none: it reads as sloppy, and at volume it's a bill. So "already sent" lives as a label on the card itself, not in n8n's memory where a restart would lose it — the team can see it, and a second run finds it. The other trap is that dragging a card emits two events, one for position and one for the list change; only the second carries the list names. Skip that filter and every customer gets two texts, and you find out in production.
When it fails
Nothing fails silently, and nothing fails into a log file nobody opens. A card with no number, a malformed number, a card confirmed twice, a message Twilio rejects — each one writes a comment back onto the card, naming the reason. The person who moved the card is the person who needs to know, and the card is where they already are.
Cost to run
An n8n subscription, plus roughly €0.07 per SMS to Italy. No phone number to rent — it sends from an alphanumeric sender ID.
Not handled yet
Named on purpose, because each is a decision rather than an oversight. No retry: a failure is recorded, not re-attempted, and whether a stale reminder should go out an hour late is a business call. No delivery confirmation — Twilio accepting a message isn't the handset receiving it. No quiet hours: a card confirmed at 2am sends at 2am. No opt-out handling, which becomes a hard requirement once volume is real.
Same shape, different edges
The architecture is: an event in a system of record triggers a message, and the outcome is written back to the record. Swap the trigger for a Calendar event, an Airtable status, a CRM stage change, a form submission or a Stripe payment. Swap the channel for WhatsApp, email, or a Slack message to staff. Or change the timing — a reminder 24 hours before instead of on confirmation. The two clients ask for next are two-way replies, where the customer texts back YES and the card moves itself, and escalation, where no reply in two hours tells a human to call.
